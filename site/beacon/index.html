      How the beacon calculator works

      A beacon sits on a stepped pyramid of iron, gold, emerald, diamond or netherite blocks, and you can mix them freely. Each level adds a bigger layer underneath: 3×3, then 5×5, 7×7 and 9×9. A full level 4 pyramid takes 164 blocks. Every block in each square must be there, so the pyramid can't be hollow.

      Which blocks should I use?

      Any of the five work, and the block type doesn't change the range or the powers, so pick the cheapest one you have. Iron is the usual choice, since an iron farm makes it easy to get. Each block takes 9 ingots or gems. Gold, emerald and diamond only change how the pyramid looks, and netherite costs far more for the same result.

      How far does a beacon reach?

      The range is 20 blocks at level 1 and grows by 10 per level, up to 50 at level 4. It reaches that far to each side and that far below the beacon, and all the way up to the top of the world. Effects last a few seconds after you leave the range: 11 at level 1, up to 17 at level 4.

      How do I turn a beacon on?

      Craft it from 5 glass, 3 obsidian and a nether star, place it on top of the pyramid, and make sure it can see the sky. Glass and other see-through blocks above it are fine, and stained glass tints the beam. Then open it and pay one iron ingot, gold ingot, emerald, diamond or netherite ingot to pick a power.

      What does level 4 add?

      Level 4 unlocks a secondary power: Regeneration, or level II of the primary power you picked. Level II Speed, Haste, Resistance, Jump Boost or Strength is often the better choice.
